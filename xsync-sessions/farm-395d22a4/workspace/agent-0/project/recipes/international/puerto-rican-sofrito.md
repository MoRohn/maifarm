# Puerto Rican Sofrito

## Description
Sofrito is the aromatic base of Puerto Rican cuisine - a vibrant green sauce made from fresh herbs, peppers, and aromatics. This raw blend is the secret ingredient that gives authentic flavor to countless Puerto Rican dishes.

## Ingredients

### Essential Ingredients:
- 2 large green bell peppers, seeded and quartered
- 6-8 ají dulce peppers (or 1 red bell pepper as substitute)
- 2 medium onions, quartered
- 1 whole head of garlic, peeled (10-12 cloves)
- 2 bunches fresh cilantro (culantro if available)
- 1 bunch fresh oregano leaves

### Optional Additions:
- 4-6 sweet mini peppers
- 2 Italian frying peppers (cubanelle)
- 1 small bunch fresh basil
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon salt

## Instructions

### Preparation (15 minutes):
1. **Clean all vegetables:**
   - Wash and dry all peppers
   - Remove stems, seeds, and membranes
   - Peel garlic cloves
   - Wash herbs and remove tough stems

2. **Rough chop ingredients:**
   - Quarter peppers and onions
   - Leave garlic cloves whole
   - Roughly chop cilantro

### Blending (5 minutes):
1. **Layer in food processor:**
   - Start with garlic and onions
   - Add peppers
   - Top with herbs

2. **Process the mixture:**
   - Pulse 10-15 times to break down
   - Process continuously for 1-2 minutes
   - Scrape sides as needed
   - Blend until smooth but not liquified
   - Should have a chunky salsa consistency

3. **Optional oil addition:**
   - Drizzle olive oil while processing
   - Helps preserve color and texture

## Uses in Puerto Rican Cooking

### Classic Dishes:
- **Arroz con gandules** (Rice with pigeon peas)
- **Pernil** (Roasted pork shoulder)
- **Sancocho** (Hearty stew)
- **Mofongo** (Fried plantain dish)
- **Pasteles** (Traditional tamales)

### How to Use:
- Sauté 2-3 tablespoons as base for rice dishes
- Marinate meats with sofrito overnight
- Add to beans while cooking
- Mix into ground meat for empanadas
- Stir into soups and stews

## Storage Methods

### Refrigerator Storage:
- Pour into glass jar with tight lid
- Cover surface with thin layer of olive oil
- Keeps fresh for 2-3 weeks
- Always use clean spoon to prevent contamination

### Freezer Storage:
- **Ice cube method:**
  - Pour into ice cube trays
  - Freeze solid, then transfer to bags
  - Each cube equals ~2 tablespoons
  - Keeps for 6 months

- **Jar method:**
  - Leave 1 inch headspace for expansion
  - Freeze in small portions
  - Thaw in refrigerator when needed

## Regional Variations

### Dominican Sofrito:
- Includes tomatoes and bell peppers
- Often has vinegar added

### Cuban Sofrito:
- Cooked version with tomato sauce
- Includes bay leaves

### Traditional Puerto Rican:
- Always raw, never cooked before storing
- No tomatoes in authentic version

## Chef's Tips
- Ají dulce adds authentic flavor without heat
- Culantro (recao) is traditional but cilantro works
- Make large batches when herbs are fresh
- Color should be vibrant green
- Smell should be fresh and aromatic
- Add to dishes at beginning of cooking

## Nutritional Benefits
- Rich in vitamins A and C
- Anti-inflammatory properties
- Natural flavor enhancer
- No preservatives needed
- Low in calories

## Cultural Significance
Sofrito is the heart of Puerto Rican cooking, passed down through generations. Every family has their own recipe, and making sofrito from scratch is a tradition that connects Puerto Ricans to their heritage.
